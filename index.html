BBQMath

Honest low-and-slow math. Cook hours by weight and temp, the stall explained, the rest defended, and the start time computed backwards from dinner.

Dinner decides when you light the fire. Serve at 6 with an 18-hour cook means fire at 9 the night before, with the rest and the buffer that meat always demands. The cooler hold absorbs the uncertainty; guests absorb nothing.
Yield is the number everyone gets wrong. A brisket keeps about 55% of its raw weight after trim and rendering. Feeding 10 people at half a pound cooked means buying 9 pounds, not 5.
The stall is not a crisis. Hours parked at 150-170F while evaporation does its work - wrap to push through or wait and keep the bark. Probe-tender beats the thermometer number, and the rest is non-negotiable.
